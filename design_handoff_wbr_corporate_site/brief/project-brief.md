## Other Notes

##### Event Photography - Keywords and Feedback (edited by Claude)

Reviewed all photos in the Event Photography folder for the emotional/energy read, not just literal content — the goal being to inform how the rebrand's design language (color, mood, tone) should feel.

**Two distinct energies show up, split by moment type:**

- Networking/reception shots (rooftop receptions, hallway chats, bar moments) read warm and human — golden-hour or amber indoor light, genuine unposed laughter, drinks in hand, close intimate crops. Locations lean aspirational (Monaco skyline, Miami palm trees, string-lit patios). This is where the "friendly, community" feeling the case study is chasing already exists in the raw photography.
- Stage/session shots (panels, keynotes, general sessions) read cooler and moodier — deep purples, magentas, and blues dominate, often with dramatic low-key lighting and large dark auditoriums. Expressions here are focused/serious rather than warm. This is where the site's current "business, buttoned-up" feeling is coming from.

**On color specifically:** the stage photography is genuinely darker and more saturated than you'd expect from a typical corporate blue palette — the newer sub-brands (iGX, LogiPharma) lean into hot pink/purple/magenta gradients even in formal panel settings, not just navy. That's a stronger, bolder palette than the current corporate site style guide, and it lines up with the brief's note that recent rebrands are pushing bolder — it may make the green Connect (#33aa99) and blue Insights (#03aeef) additions feel more at home than they would against a flatter, more traditional blue.

**Design language implication:** the two energies don't need to be reconciled into one flat tone — they can be used deliberately. Hero/homepage imagery pulled from the warm candid networking shots would push the "community, friendly, youthful" goal harder than stage photography would. Stage/session photography, with its moodier saturated color, could work well as secondary/background imagery or texture (e.g., section dividers, dark-mode panels) where the current site relies on gradients and box shadows instead of real photography — since those shots are already naturally dark, they may need less added shadow to make text legible, addressing the "heavy dark box shadow" complaint directly.

##### Pre-Design Brief Check (Claude notes)

Ran through the brief looking for gaps before this moves to Claude design. Leo's answers below, resolved:

- **Bootstrap version:** The corporate site and the event sites are hosted separately, which is why they're on different Bootstrap versions. Corporate site's current baseline stays as documented (4.2.1) for the audit; the redesign is upgrading it, in line with the stated goal of moving to the latest version.
- **Mobile-first:** Not explicitly stated in the brief before, but confirmed as the approach. Design should be mobile-first, with breakpoints/collapsing behavior left to Claude design to figure out — upgrading to a newer Bootstrap grid should make this collapse more cleanly than the current version does.
- **Content inventory:** Claude design should take the elements that already exist on the live conferences page (filters, event cards, feed layout, etc.) as the working content structure, rather than inventing new data fields from scratch.
- **Logo:** Use the full WBR logo — wordmark + globe mark, without "Worldwide Business Research" spelled out underneath (i.e. `wbr-acro.jpg`, not `wbr-full.jpg`).
- **Audience:** Majority of visitors are repeat conference attendees, but design should still account for first-time visitors landing cold. That said, the homepage should not over-explain "who we are" — that positioning is intentional and gets addressed on the About pages later, not here.
- **CMS constraints:** The corporate site's CMS is more flexible than ECO2 (used for the event sites) — full control over markup, no special constraints to design around.
- **Conferences URL routing:** The hash-based routing (`#/`) is just how the live event feed currently works and isn't something to address in this redesign.
